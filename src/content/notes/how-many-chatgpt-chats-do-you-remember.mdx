I can remember having a useful conversation with ChatGPT. I usually cannot remember which chat it was in.

I use ChatGPT to think, write, plan, debug, and occasionally explain something I should probably already know. Some of those chats contain useful decisions. Then I close the tab and forget they existed. I remember the feeling of a useful chat more often than I remember the chat itself.

This is especially annoying because I have ADHD, low dopamine, and poor working memory in some areas. I can remember that I solved a problem. Finding the solution again is a different boss fight.

## Try a small test

Look at five old ChatGPT conversations. Before opening them, how many do you recognise from the title?

I did this and realised that my chat history had become a very large cupboard full of unnamed cables. Something in there may be useful. I do not want to inspect every cable.

## The answer exists. I just cannot find it

ChatGPT can often answer the question in front of me. The problem is that the useful context may be trapped in another conversation, another app, or a note I wrote six months ago.

So I keep repeating myself.

I explain the project again. I describe the decision again. I search for the old chat. Sometimes I find it. Sometimes I start a new chat and pretend the old one never existed.

That last option is surprisingly effective until I need the old decision.

## I kept building places to store things

I have tried the normal solutions. Notes apps. Project pages. Databases. A few systems with enough tags to qualify as a minor government department.

The pattern was always the same. I built the system, enjoyed the feeling of having a system, and then stopped maintaining it.

I had plenty of places to store things. I kept forgetting to use them.

Then I had to remember where I put something when I needed it.

## The export plan was not the answer

At one point I thought I should export my old conversations and put everything into one searchable system.

This sounded sensible for about ten minutes.

Then I looked at the exports. Different formats. Messy data. Conversations that were useful for five minutes and irrelevant forever after. I had a large cleanup job before I had proved that the result would help.

I would also have to decide what deserved to survive. Every conversation? Only the important ones? How would I know before I needed them?

I stopped asking how to import everything and started asking whether the assistant could find one useful thing.

> What if the assistant could reach the relevant context when I needed it?

I did not need another place to dump everything. I needed a way to find the one thing I had already decided when I needed it again.

I started calling that place Life OS. It holds the decisions and context I would be annoyed to lose.

## The assistant still needs a way to look

Imagine asking:

> What did I decide about Japan?

The assistant may understand the question. The answer could still be somewhere else.

That is the part I care about. I want useful context without handing over an invisible copy of everything I have ever written.

## Where MCP fits

MCP stands for Model Context Protocol. The name is less interesting than the job it does.

It gives an assistant a standard way to ask an approved service for information. In this example, the assistant asks for a note. The service searches the connected context and returns a result.

The path looks like this:

```text
Your question -> approved connection -> saved context -> useful answer
```

## Stored information is not useful memory

Saving a note is only the first step. The system still needs to find the right note, decide whether it fits the question, and let me correct it when it is wrong.

For example, I might write:

> I decided to focus my Japan trip on food and hiking.

Later, that decision should appear when I ask about planning the trip. I should also be able to keep it, edit it, or reject it.

I called that retrieval layer Total Recall. It is a movie reference, a bad pun, and a name I can actually remember.

The name is less important than the rule. The system should help me find a decision when I need it. It should not quietly turn every stray thought into permanent memory.

## What I am trying to build

The assistant asks for the note. The connection carries the request. The note comes back.

I want to ask a question without reconstructing six months of my own thinking first.

I am still working out how much of this should exist as a product and how much is me building infrastructure to avoid searching one old chat.

Both explanations may be true.

The useful result would be simple. I ask a question, the assistant finds the context I meant, and I do not have to reconstruct six months of my own thinking first.

Finding the note is the problem I am solving first.

The harder problem is deciding what deserves to be remembered, what has become outdated, and when the assistant should ask me before using it.
